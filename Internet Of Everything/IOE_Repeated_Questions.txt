RANK 1 [REPEATED IN 6 OUT OF 6 PAPERS - 100% OCCURRENCE]
TOPIC: MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport) Architecture & Working
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Marks: 10 Marks
Total Appearances: 6 Times
Appeared in:
  • 04/06/2025 (Q.3a)
  • 14/11/2024 (Q.4b)
  • NOV 2024 / 2025 (Q.4b)
  • JUNE 2024 (Q.5a)
  • 28-DEC-2023 (Q.5a)
  • 16/06/2023 (Q.6a)

Exact Question Variations:
1. "Draw and explain Architecture of MQTT with diagram. List its advantages." (Jun 2025 Q.3a, Nov 2024 Q.4b, Jun 2023 Q.6a)
2. "Elaborate MQTT with its working in details and two advantages over COAP." (Dec 2023 Q.5a, Jun 2024 Q.5a)

What Examiners Look For:
  • Publish / Subscribe Architecture Diagram: Publishers (Sensors/Clients) -> Central MQTT Broker -> Subscribers (Applications/Actuators).
  • MQTT Packet Structure: Fixed Header (2 bytes), Variable Header, Payload.
  • Quality of Service (QoS) Levels:
      - QoS 0: At most once delivery (Fire and forget, no ACK).
      - QoS 1: At least once delivery (Guaranteed delivery, PUBACK, potential duplicates).
      - QoS 2: Exactly once delivery (4-step handshake: PUBLISH -> PUBREC -> PUBREL -> PUBCOMP).
  • Advanced Features: Topics hierarchy (`home/kitchen/temp`), Wildcards (`+` single level, `#` multi level), Retained Messages, Last Will & Testament (LWT), Keep-Alive ping.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Marks: 10 Marks (and 5M)

Appeared in:

  • 16/06/2023 (Q.2a - 10M)

Exact Question Variations:
"Compare and contrast Edge, Fog, and Cloud Computing. Explain their usage with an example / Differentiate and give Hierarchy between Edge, Fog, and Cloud computing."

What Examiners Look For:
  • 3-Tier Hierarchy Diagram:
      - Bottom Tier (Edge Layer): End sensors, actuators, microcontrollers (ESP32/Arduino). Sub-millisecond latency, localized immediate filtering.
      - Middle Tier (Fog Layer): Local gateways, routers, edge servers. Millisecond latency, localized aggregation, protocol translation, offline survivability.
      - Top Tier (Cloud Layer): Centralized data centers (AWS, Azure). High latency (seconds), massive storage, historical big data analytics, deep ML model training.
  • Comparison Table: Latency, Processing Power, Storage, Network Bandwidth usage, Security/Privacy, Deployment location.

RANK 3 [REPEATED IN 5 OUT OF 6 PAPERS - 83% OCCURRENCE]
TOPIC: CoAP (Constrained Application Protocol) Architecture & Working
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Marks: 10 Marks (and 5M)
Total Appearances: 5 Times
Appeared in:
  • 04/06/2025 (Q.3b & Q.4b)
  • 14/11/2024 (Q.2b)
  • NOV 2024 / 2025 (Q.2b)
  • JUNE 2024 (Q.1b - 5M)
  • 16/06/2023 (Q.5b)

Exact Question Variations:
"Explain working of Constrained Application Layer protocol (CoAP) / Explain the terms CoAP with its architecture."

What Examiners Look For:
  • Protocol Stack: CoAP operates over UDP (port 5683) to eliminate TCP 3-way handshake connection overhead.
  • RESTful Model: Uses GET, POST, PUT, DELETE methods with URIs (`coap://sensor.local/temp`).
  • 4 Message Types:
      1. Confirmable (CON): Requires an Acknowledgment (ACK).
      2. Non-Confirmable (NON): Fire-and-forget message.
      3. Acknowledgment (ACK): Confirms receipt of CON.
      4. Reset (RST): Rejects unparseable/aborted message.
  • Features: Resource observation (Pub/Sub extension RFC 7641), Block-wise transfer, DTLS security.

RANK 5 [REPEATED IN 4 OUT OF 6 PAPERS - 67% OCCURRENCE]
TOPIC: LoRa and LoRaWAN Architecture, Characteristics & Device Classes
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Marks: 10 Marks
Total Appearances: 4 Times
Appeared in:
  • 04/06/2025 (Q.5a)
  • 14/11/2024 (Q.3a)
  • JUNE 2024 (Q.2b)
  • 16/06/2023 (Q.3b)

Exact Question Variations:
"Explain the architecture of LoRaWAN with its major characteristics / Evaluate long-range communication systems and protocols such as LoRa and LoRaWAN."

What Examiners Look For:
  • Physical Layer (LoRa) vs MAC/Network Layer (LoRaWAN): Chirp Spread Spectrum (CSS) modulation, ISM unlicensed bands (868 MHz / 915 MHz).
  • 4-Tier Network Architecture Diagram: End Devices (Nodes) -> LoRa Gateways (Concentrators) -> Network Server -> Application Server.
  • 3 End-Device Classes:
      - Class A (All): Battery powered, uplink-triggered two short downlink receive windows (Lowest power).
      - Class B (Beacon): Scheduled synchronized downlink receive slots via periodic beacons.
      - Class C (Continuous): Continuously listening receiver, lowest downlink latency, mains-powered.

TOPIC: AMQP (Advanced Message Queuing Protocol) Architecture
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Marks: 10 Marks (and 5M)
Total Appearances: 4 Times
Appeared in:
  • 04/06/2025 (Q.1c - 5M)
  • 14/11/2024 (Q.5b - 10M)
  • NOV 2024 / 2025 (Q.5b - 10M)
  • 16/06/2023 (Q.1c - 5M)

Exact Question Variations:
"Describe the Architecture of Advanced Message Queuing Protocol (AMQP) with major application / Draw architecture diagram of AMQP with advantages."

What Examiners Look For:
  • Architecture Diagram: Publisher -> Message Broker (Exchange -> Bindings -> Queues) -> Consumer.
  • 4 Types of Exchanges:
      1. Direct Exchange: Routes message based on exact routing key match.
      2. Fanout Exchange: Broadcasts message to all bound queues unconditionally.
      3. Topic Exchange: Pattern-based routing with wildcards (`*` single word, `#` zero or more words).
      4. Headers Exchange: Routes based on message header attributes instead of routing key.

Exact Question Variations:
"Describe the role of NoSQL in IoT Data Analytics Challenges / Explain the role of NoSQL in IoT data analytics."

What Examiners Look For:
  • IoT Data Characteristics (The 5 V's: Volume, Velocity, Variety, Veracity, Value).
  • Why Relational DBs Fail: Schema rigidity, poor horizontal scaling, bottleneck under high write ingestion rates.
  • 4 Types of NoSQL in IoT:
      1. Time-Series DBs (InfluxDB, TimescaleDB - optimal for timestamped sensor logs).
      2. Document Stores (MongoDB, Couchbase - flexible JSON sensor payloads).
      3. Key-Value Stores (Redis - high-throughput edge caching).
      4. Wide-Column Stores (Apache Cassandra - distributed horizontal write scaling).
