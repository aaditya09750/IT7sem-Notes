## 2. Tier 1: Ultra High Frequency (4/5 Papers - 80% Recurrence)

### [IRS-01] Inverted Index Construction & Block Addressing Optimization
- **Repetition Frequency:** **4 out of 5 Papers (80% Recurrence)**
- **Exam Appearances:** Nov 2025 (Q.4a), Dec 2024 (Q.4b), Jun 2024 (Q.5b), Jun 2023 (Q.5a)
- **MUSA Reference:** Question Bank **Q.1**
- **Typical Marks:** 10 Marks

#### Exact Question Formulations:
> "Describe the process of creating an inverted index with an example. How can this process be optimized using block addressing?" / "Describe creating an inverted index without stop words for: 'This is a text. A text has many words. Words are made from letters.'"

#### Examiner Expected Answers Checklist:
- **Core Concept:** An index data structure mapping words to their locations in a document collection.
- **Components:**
  1. *Vocabulary / Dictionary:* Sorted list of distinct terms.
  2. *Postings List:* Linked list or array of document pointers (DocID, term frequency $tf$, positional offsets).
- **Step-by-Step Construction Trace:**
  - Tokenization $\to$ Stop-word removal $\to$ Stemming $\to$ Term-Doc pairs generation $\to$ Sorting $\to$ Postings aggregation.
- **Block Addressing Optimization (Crucial for Full Marks):**
  - Instead of storing exact 64-bit byte pointers to words, the text corpus is partitioned into fixed blocks (e.g., 64 KB).
  - The index points only to the Block ID.
  - *Benefits:* Reduces index storage space by 50% to 80%; allows in-memory caching of the vocabulary.

### [IRS-02] User Relevance Feedback & Rocchio Algorithm Formulation
- **Repetition Frequency:** **4 out of 5 Papers (80% Recurrence)**
- **Exam Appearances:** Nov 2025 (Q.6a), Dec 2024 (Q.6a), Jun 2024 (Q.2a), Jun 2023 (Q.2a)
- **MUSA Reference:** Question Bank **Q.2**
- **Typical Marks:** 10 Marks / 5 Marks

#### Examiner Expected Answers Checklist:
- **Concept:** Iterative query reformulation based on user marking of returned documents as relevant or non-relevant.
- **Rocchio Algorithm Formula (Mandatory):**
  $$\vec{q}_m = \alpha \vec{q}_0 + \beta \frac{1}{|D_r|} \sum_{\vec{d}_j \in D_r} \vec{d}_j - \gamma \frac{1}{|D_{nr}|} \sum_{\vec{d}_k \in D_{nr}} \vec{d}_k$$
- **Parameters Explained:**
  - $\vec{q}_0$: Original query vector.
  - $\vec{q}_m$: Modified / expanded query vector.
  - $D_r$: Set of relevant documents identified by user.
  - $D_{nr}$: Set of non-relevant documents.
  - $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$: Tuning weights (Standard SMART values: $\alpha = 8, \beta = 16, \gamma = 4$).
- **Feedback Types:** Explicit Feedback, Implicit Feedback (clicks, dwell time), Pseudo-Relevance (Blind) Feedback.

### [IRS-03] Keyword-Based Queries (Phrase, Proximity, Wildcard Queries)
- **Repetition Frequency:** **4 out of 5 Papers (80% Recurrence)**
- **Exam Appearances:** Nov 2025 (Q.5a), Dec 2024 (Q.2b), Jun 2024 (Q.3b), Jun 2023 (Q.4b)
- **MUSA Reference:** Question Bank **Q.3**
- **Typical Marks:** 10 Marks / 5 Marks

#### Technical Requirements:
1. **Single-Word Queries:** Exact lexical lookup in inverted index vocabulary.
2. **Phrase Queries:** Sequence of terms that must appear contiguously (e.g., `"data science"`). Solved via:
   - *Biword Index:* Indexing adjacent pairs of words.
   - *Positional Inverted Index:* Postings store exact token positions $(doc_1: \text{pos}_3, \text{pos}_7)$.
3. **Proximity Queries:** Terms must occur within $k$ words of each other (`"cloud" NEAR/5 "security"`). Uses positional intersection algorithm.
4. **Wildcard Queries:**
   - *Prefix / Trailing Wildcard (`data*`):* Solved via B-tree.
   - *Leading / Infix Wildcard (`*science`, `d*ta`):* Solved via Permuterm Index or $k$-gram Index ($k=3$).

### [IRS-04] Natural Language Modeling Issues in Information Retrieval
- **Repetition Frequency:** **4 out of 5 Papers (80% Recurrence)**
- **Exam Appearances:** Jun 2025 (Q.2a), Nov 2025 (Q.3b), Dec 2024 (Q.3a), Jun 2024 (Q.6b)
- **MUSA Reference:** Question Bank **Q.4**
- **Typical Marks:** 10 Marks / 5 Marks

#### Core Theoretical Issues & Solutions:
- **Polysemy:** A single word having multiple distinct meanings (e.g., "Apple" = fruit vs company). Lowers **Precision**.
  - *Solution:* Word Sense Disambiguation (WSD), contextual embeddings (BERT).
- **Synonymy:** Multiple words expressing the exact same concept (e.g., "car" and "automobile"). Lowers **Recall**.
  - *Solution:* Query expansion, Thesaurus lookup, Latent Semantic Indexing (LSI).
- **Morphological Variations:** Verb tenses, plurals. Solved via Porter Stemming and Lemmatization.
- **Syntactic Ambiguity:** Sentence structure confusion (e.g., "saw the man with binoculars").

## 3. Tier 2: Very High Frequency (3/5 Papers - 60% Recurrence)

### [IRS-08] Vector Space Model (VSM) & Cosine Similarity Ranking (Numerical)
- **Repetition Frequency:** **3 out of 5 Papers (60% Recurrence)**
- **Exam Appearances:** Jun 2025 (Q.3a), Nov 2025 (Q.2a), Jun 2024 (Q.3a)
- **MUSA Reference:** Question Bank **Q.8** & **Numerical 1**
- **Typical Marks:** 10 Marks
- **Core Formulae:**
  - Term Frequency: $tf_{t,d} = \text{frequency of term } t \text{ in document } d$.
  - Inverse Document Frequency: $idf_t = \log_{10}\left(\frac{N}{df_t}\right)$ where $N = \text{total docs}, df_t = \text{document frequency}$.
  - Weight: $w_{t,d} = tf_{t,d} \times idf_t$.
  - Cosine Similarity:
    $$\text{Sim}(d, q) = \frac{\vec{d} \cdot \vec{q}}{\|\vec{d}\| \|\vec{q}\|} = \frac{\sum w_{t,d} \cdot w_{t,q}}{\sqrt{\sum w_{t,d}^2} \sqrt{\sum w_{t,q}^2}}$$

## 5. Tier 4: Single Appearance & Core Syllabus Questions

1. **PageRank Algorithm (MUSA QB Q.21 - 10M):**
   $$PR(A) = (1 - d) + d \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{PR(T_i)}{C(T_i)}$$
   - Random surfer model, damping factor $d = 0.85$, handling dead ends and spider traps.
2. **HITS Algorithm (Hubs and Authorities - 10M):**
   - Mutually reinforcing relationship: good hubs point to good authorities; authority score $a(p) = \sum h(q)$, hub score $h(p) = \sum a(q)$.
3. **Okapi BM25 Ranking Model (MUSA QB Q.22 - 10M):**
   - Non-linear term frequency saturation parameter $k_1$, document length normalization $b$.
4. **Boyer-Moore String Matching (MUSA Numerical 2 - 10M):**
   - Right-to-left character comparison; Bad Character heuristic: $\text{Shift} = \max(1, j - \text{last}(c))$.

## 6. MUSA Mandatory Section: Tabular Differentiations

### 1. Information Retrieval (IR) vs Data Retrieval (DR)
| Parameter / Basis | Information Retrieval (IR) | Data Retrieval (DR) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Data Nature** | Unstructured, semi-structured natural language text | Highly structured tables, relational databases |
| **Query Formulation** | Vague, ambiguous keywords, natural language | Precise query languages (SQL, relational algebra) |
| **Matching Logic** | Probabilistic, partial match, similarity scoring | Exact Boolean match ($True / False$) |
| **Result Presentation**| Ranked list ordered by relevance score | Unordered set of all records satisfying condition |
| **Error Consequence** | Tolerates small error (acceptable noise in ranking)| Zero tolerance; missing record is fatal error |

### 2. Suffix Tree vs Suffix Array
| Parameter / Basis | Suffix Tree | Suffix Array |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Data Structure** | Compressed trie containing all suffixes of text | Array of integers representing sorted suffixes |
| **Space Complexity** | High memory overhead ($10\times \text{to } 20\times$ text size) | Space efficient ($4\times \text{text size}$) |
| **Pattern Search Time**| $O(m)$ where $m$ is pattern length | $O(m \log n)$ via binary search (or $O(m)$ with LCP) |
| **Implementation** | Complex pointer manipulation (Ukkonen's algorithm)| Simple array sorting |

### 3. Pre-coordination vs Post-coordination
| Parameter / Basis | Pre-coordination Indexing | Post-coordination Indexing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Coordination Point** | Terms are joined during index creation stage | Terms are combined by searcher at query time |
| **Example** | Printed library subject catalog (`"India - History - 1947"`) | Modern search engine queries (`"India" AND "History"`) |
| **User Flexibility** | Low; user must search prescribed compound heading | High; unlimited combination of keywords |

## 7. MUSA Mandatory Section: High-Yield Short Notes

1. **Porter's Stemming Algorithm:**
   - 5-step heuristic stripping common English suffixes (`-ed`, `-ing`, `-ly`, `-tion`) based on vowel-consonant measure $m$.
2. **Web Crawling & Robots.txt:**
   - Client-side policy standard allowing webmasters to specify which paths automated crawlers are forbidden to access (`Disallow: /admin/`).
3. **11-Point Interpolated Precision-Recall Graph:**
   - Standard TREC evaluation curve evaluating precision across recall checkpoints $\{0.0, 0.1, \dots, 1.0\}$ to compare different IR systems.
4. **Stopword Filtering:**
   - Removing high-frequency words carrying little semantic value ("the", "is", "at") to save up to 40% index storage.
5. **Okapi BM25 Ranking:**
   - Probabilistic TF-IDF alternative preventing extreme term frequency domination via asymptotic saturation.

## 8. Compulsory Numericals & Algorithmic Traces

### Vector Space Model Document Ranking (10-Mark Numerical Blueprint)
**Problem Template:**
Given a vocabulary of terms $\{T_1, T_2, T_3\}$ across 3 Documents and 1 Query:
- Total Documents $N = 3$.
- Query contains terms: $q = \{T_1, T_2\}$.

**Execution Steps for Full Marks:**
1. Calculate $df_t$ (number of documents containing term $t$).
2. Compute $idf_t = \log_{10}(N / df_t)$.
3. Construct Term-Document Weight Matrix: $w_{t,d} = tf_{t,d} \times idf_t$.
4. Calculate Euclidean Lengths: $\|\vec{d}\| = \sqrt{\sum w_{t,d}^2}, \quad \|\vec{q}\| = \sqrt{\sum w_{t,q}^2}$.
5. Calculate Cosine Similarity: $\text{Sim}(d_i, q) = \frac{\sum w_{t,d_i} \cdot w_{t,q}}{\|\vec{d_i}\| \cdot \|\vec{q}\|}$.
6. Rank documents in descending order of similarity score.
